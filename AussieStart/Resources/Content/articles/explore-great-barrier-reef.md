# Overview
From Cairns or Port Douglas you can day-trip to Great Barrier Reef pontoons, snorkel sites, or island hops.

# Why this matters
Bucket-list nature — but weather, reef health advisories, and seasickness planning matter.

# When to do it
Dry season (generally May–October) is popular for clearer seas and fewer storms. Always check stinger season precautions.

# Typical fees (AUD, approx.)
- **Flights to Cairns (domestic return)**: $200–$550+ pp
- **Outer reef day cruise (pontoon / snorkel)**: $220–$350 pp typically
- **Introductory scuba add-on**: $100–$160 extra
- **Green Island transfers + island day**: often $100–$160 pp range
- **Reef Tax / EMC environmental charges**: commonly bundled or ~$6–$20 — confirm on booking
- **Wetsuit / prescription mask hire**: $10–$30
- **Port Douglas premium reef trips**: often $280–$400 pp

# Short itinerary (2–3 days in Cairns)
**Day 1:** Arrive, Esplanade walk, book/confirm reef trip, early night  
**Day 2:** Full outer-reef day cruise (leave ~7–8am, return late afternoon)  
**Day 3 (optional):** Kuranda scenic rail/skyrail **or** Palm Cove recovery morning

# Stops to explore in between
- **Cairns Esplanade lagoon** (free swim)
- **Rusty’s Markets** (weekends)
- **Kuranda village** — markets + Barron Falls views
- **Palm Cove** — quieter beach strip
- **Port Douglas** — if based further north
- **Barron Gorge** lookouts on the Kuranda corridor

# Common mistakes
- Booking cheapest trip without checking inclusions (lunch, snorkel gear, reef tax)
- Skipping seasickness tablets if you get queasy
- Ignoring stinger suit advice in season

# FAQs
**Can non-swimmers go?** Many pontoons have underwater observatories and glass-bottom boats — confirm before booking.
**Is the reef “dead”?** Conditions vary by site and season; operators choose workable locations daily.

# Useful terms
- **Pontoon**: Floating platform moored on the reef
- **EMC**: Environmental Management Charge
