# Overview
Car insurance in Australia is **not one product**. **CTP** (compulsory third party) is required to register a vehicle. Comprehensive and third-party property are extra, sold by insurers. AussieStart does not sell policies. Compare using [Moneysmart — car insurance](https://moneysmart.gov.au/car-insurance).

# Why this matters
CTP mainly covers **injuries to other people**. It usually does **not** pay to repair your car after you hit a pole, or another driver’s bumper. Newcomers often buy a car with CTP only and discover that after a small crash.

# When to do it
Before you drive a car you own or are buying — the same day as registration / CTP, not “next month”.

# Requirements
- Vehicle details (rego / VIN)
- Licence details
- Time to read excess, exclusions, and whether the driver is listed

# Step-by-step guide
1. Read the government consumer page: [Car insurance (Moneysmart)](https://moneysmart.gov.au/car-insurance).
2. Arrange **CTP** through the official {{state.short}} channel (it is part of registration in most states). You cannot skip it and drive legally.
3. Decide whether you also need **third-party property** (damage you cause to other cars/property) and/or **comprehensive** (your car + others). That is a private purchase — use the insurer’s own site.
4. Check excess, named drivers, windscreen, and hire-car after a claim. Do not buy from a cold-call SMS.
5. If you buy a used car, start CTP **before** you drive it away. See the PPSR guide for finance checks.

# Common mistakes
- Assuming “I paid CTP so the car is fully insured”
- Letting a friend drive who is not listed
- Buying comprehensive from a lookalike website

# FAQs
**Is CTP the same as comprehensive?** No.
**Can AussieStart issue a certificate of insurance?** No.

# Useful terms
- **CTP**: Compulsory third party (injury to others)
- **Third-party property**: Damage to other people’s cars/property
- **Comprehensive**: Usually your vehicle plus third-party property
- **Excess**: What you pay on a claim
