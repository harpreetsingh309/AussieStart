# Overview
An Australian bank account is essential for wages, rent, and everyday spending.

# Why this matters
Employers and Centrelink (if eligible) usually need local BSB and account details.

# When to do it
Within your first 1–3 days.

# Requirements
Typical documents (banks vary):
- Passport
- Visa grant / evidence of stay
- Australian address (even temporary)
- Sometimes a second ID or student enrolment letter

# Step-by-step guide
1. Compare big banks and neo-banks for foreign-ID support.
2. Book an in-branch appointment if online onboarding rejects overseas ID.
3. Open a transaction account first.
4. Activate debit card / digital wallet.
5. Set up PayID with your mobile number once ready.

# Common mistakes
- Waiting weeks and getting paid into an overseas account with high fees
- Sharing OTPs with anyone who calls claiming to be the bank
- Ignoring monthly fees on the wrong account type

# FAQs
**What is BSB?** A 6-digit bank/state/branch code used with your account number.
**What is PayID?** A payment address linked to your phone/email.

# Useful terms
- **Osko**: Near-instant transfers between participating banks
- **Debit card**: Spends your own money from the account
