# Overview
Calling **000** is always correct in a life-threatening emergency. Paying for the **ambulance ride** is a separate question and **differs by state**. AussieStart does not sell memberships. Start with [healthdirect — ambulance costs and cover](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/ambulance-costs-and-cover).

# Why this matters
In some states a resident is covered. In others you may receive a large invoice unless you have ambulance membership, private health that includes ambulance, or another listed concession. Visitors are often billed even where locals are not.

# When to do it
In the first weeks after you have an address in {{state.name}} — **before** you need a non-emergency transfer. Do not delay calling 000 because of cost.

# Requirements
- Your state and whether you are a resident, student, or visitor
- Medicare / OSHC / OVHC / private health details if you have them
- Time to read the **state ambulance** page, not a forum rumour

# Step-by-step guide
1. Call **000** first if someone is in danger. Cost is not the decision.
2. Read the national explainer: [Ambulance costs and cover (healthdirect)](https://www.healthdirect.gov.au/ambulance-costs-and-cover).
3. Open the **{{state.short}}** ambulance page below and confirm who is billed.
4. If your state uses **membership** or private extras for ambulance, join only on the official ambulance or health-fund site.
5. Keep membership numbers with your Medicare/OSHC card photos (offline).

## Victoria
Victorian residents are generally covered for emergency ambulance. Confirm on [Ambulance Victoria](https://www.ambulance.vic.gov.au). Visitors and some non-residents may still be billed — read their fees page.
<!-- /state -->

<!-- state:nsw -->
## NSW
NSW does **not** treat ambulance as automatically free for everyone. See [NSW Ambulance](https://www.ambulance.nsw.gov.au) for membership and fees. Private health extras sometimes include ambulance — read your PDS.
<!-- /state -->

<!-- state:qld -->
## Queensland
Queensland residents with Medicare are generally covered by the Queensland Ambulance Service. Confirm: [ambulance.qld.gov.au](https://www.ambulance.qld.gov.au). Interstate visitors and some visa holders may be charged.
<!-- /state -->

<!-- state:sa -->
## South Australia
SA Ambulance Service explains resident cover and subscriptions: [sa.gov.au ambulance](https://www.sa.gov.au/topics/health-and-wellbeing/health-services/ambulance-and-emergency). Verify if you are a visitor.
<!-- /state -->

<!-- state:wa -->
## Western Australia
St John WA is the contracted ambulance service. WA residents are generally covered; visitors may pay. Start at [St John WA](https://stjohnwa.com.au) and [wa.gov.au](https://www.wa.gov.au).
<!-- /state -->

<!-- state:tas -->
## Tasmania
Ambulance Tasmania resident cover is explained via [health.tas.gov.au](https://www.health.tas.gov.au) / Ambulance Tasmania pages. Visitors should confirm fees before assuming cover.
<!-- /state -->

<!-- state:act -->
## ACT
ACT Ambulance Service may invoice. Membership and fees: [ESA / ACT Ambulance](https://esa.act.gov.au). Do not assume NSW membership covers the ACT.
<!-- /state -->

<!-- state:nt -->
## Northern Territory
St John NT / NT Health pages explain cover. Start at [nt.gov.au](https://nt.gov.au) and confirm visitor fees.
<!-- /state -->

# Common mistakes
- Not calling 000 because you worry about a bill
- Assuming OSHC or travel insurance includes every ambulance invoice
- Buying “ambulance cover” from a Facebook ad

# FAQs
**Is 000 free to call?** Yes, the call is free. The transport invoice is a different system.
**Can AussieStart join a membership for me?** No.

# Useful terms
- **Emergency ambulance**: 000 response
- **Patient transport**: Non-emergency transfer (often billed even where emergency is covered)
