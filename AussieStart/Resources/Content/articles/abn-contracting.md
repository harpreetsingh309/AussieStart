# Overview
An ABN (Australian Business Number) is for people running a business or contracting — not for ordinary employees. This guide helps you tell the difference. AussieStart does not register ABNs or lodge tax forms.

# Why this matters
Some “jobs” that pay cash or ask you to invoice with an ABN may actually be employment. Getting this wrong can mean unpaid super, no sick pay, and tax issues.

# When to do it
Before you accept work that says “you’ll need an ABN”, especially on apps, cash jobs, or labour-hire ads.

# Requirements
- Clarity on whether you are an employee or a contractor (Fair Work / ATO have official tests)
- TFN already in progress if you will earn income
- If you truly are a business: the official ABR / ATO registration path

# Step-by-step guide
1. Read the Fair Work and ATO pages on employee vs contractor. Hours, tools, and who controls the work all matter.
2. If you are an **employee**, you should not need an ABN. You use a TFN and get payslips.
3. If you genuinely operate a business, register an ABN only on **abr.gov.au** / ATO — never through a paid “agent” DM.
4. Keep invoices, and set money aside for tax. GST registration is a separate threshold.
5. Students: confirm your visa allows self-employment before you invoice anyone.

# Common mistakes
- Paying a website that is not the official ABR to “get an ABN overnight”
- Accepting an ABN arrangement that looks like a rostered employee job
- Ignoring GST if your turnover later grows

# FAQs
**Can AussieStart apply for my ABN?** No. Only you should use the official register.
**Do I need an ABN to work at a cafe?** Usually no — that is employment.

# Useful terms
- **ABR**: Australian Business Register
- **GST**: Goods and Services Tax, registered separately if required
