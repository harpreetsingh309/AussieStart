# Overview
New arrivals are frequent targets: fake rental bonds, fake myGov or ATO messages, and “job” ads that ask for money. This guide lists patterns so you can pause and check. AussieStart never asks for passwords, codes, or payment to “approve” a visa.

# Why this matters
A single rushed payment can wipe your first-month budget. Official agencies will not demand gift cards or cryptocurrency.

# When to do it
Read this in week 1, then keep it bookmarked. Re-read before you pay a bond or send ID photos.

# Requirements
- A sceptical pause before you pay or share codes
- The official website address for the agency (always `.gov.au` for government)

# Step-by-step guide
1. If a text, WhatsApp, or call creates urgency (“your visa will be cancelled today”), stop. Hang up and use a website you typed yourself.
2. Government and banks will not ask you to move money to a “safe account”.
3. For rentals: never pay bond to a personal account before you have inspected (or done a legitimate video inspect) and have a written agreement. Use the official state bond authority when you do pay.
4. For jobs: you should not pay for a uniform, visa, or “training kit” before day one.
5. Report scams via the official Scamwatch site if you want to warn others.

# Common mistakes
- Scanning QR codes from random flyers at the airport
- Reusing the same password as your email for myGov
- Sending passport photos to a “landlord” you have never met

# FAQs
**Is 000 for scams?** No. 000 is for life-threatening emergencies. Use Scamwatch / your bank for fraud, and the police non-emergency line if you are told to.
**Can AussieStart refund a scam?** No. Contact your bank immediately.

# Useful terms
- **Phishing**: Fake email or SMS that looks official
- **Advance-fee**: A job or rental that asks for money first
