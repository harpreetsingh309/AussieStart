# Overview
Superannuation (“super”) is money set aside for retirement. If you work in Australia, your employer usually pays a percentage of your earnings into a super fund. AussieStart does not choose a fund or move money for you.

# Why this matters
Missing or split super across several funds can cost you fees. Knowing the basics helps you check your first payslip.

# When to do it
Once you have a job (including casual work). You can read this earlier so payslips make sense.

# Requirements
- TFN given to your employer (so super is taxed correctly)
- Payslips that show super contributions
- Optional: a myGov account linked to the ATO to see super later

# Step-by-step guide
1. Read your employment contract or first payslip for the super fund name and contribution amount.
2. If your employer asks you to choose a fund, compare **official** product dashboards or the fund’s own PDS — we do not recommend a specific product.
3. Keep your TFN, address, and email up to date with the fund.
4. When you change jobs, check whether you now have more than one super account.
5. For lost super, use ATO guidance inside myGov — never a random “super claim” text message.

# Common mistakes
- Ignoring the first payslip “super” line
- Clicking ads that promise to “find lost super” for a fee
- Assuming cash-in-hand jobs still pay super (often they do not — another reason to avoid them)

# FAQs
**Can AussieStart open a super account?** No. Funds and the ATO handle that.
**What if I leave Australia?** Ask the ATO about departing Australia superannuation payment (DASP) rules — eligibility depends on visa and tax residency.

# Useful terms
- **SG (Super Guarantee)**: The minimum employer contribution percentage
- **PDS**: Product Disclosure Statement for a fund
