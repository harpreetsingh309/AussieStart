# Overview
Contents insurance can cover your belongings in a rental. AussieStart does not sell policies. Compare using **Moneysmart** (government) and read the Product Disclosure Statement on the insurer’s own site.

# Why this matters
Landlord building insurance usually does **not** cover your laptop, clothes, or furniture. Share-house theft and water leaks are common first-year shocks.

# When to do it
After you have a lease address, before you move high-value items in.

# Requirements
- Address and estimated replacement value of your things
- Time to read exclusions (flood, accidental damage, share-house clauses)

# Step-by-step guide
1. Read the government explainer: [Home insurance (Moneysmart)](https://moneysmart.gov.au/home-insurance).
2. List what you would need to replace (don’t guess a single round number without a list).
3. Get quotes from insurers directly — not from a cold-call “renewal” SMS.
4. Check whether flood and portable items (phones outside the home) are included.
5. Keep the policy email with your lease and condition-report photos.

# Common mistakes
- Assuming the landlord’s policy covers tenants
- Underinsuring to save a few dollars a week
- Buying through a link in a random text

# FAQs
**Is Moneysmart an insurer?** No. [moneysmart.gov.au](https://moneysmart.gov.au) is an Australian Government ASIC site that explains products.
**Can AussieStart bind a policy?** No.
