# Overview
Your first month in Australia often costs more than you expect: SIM, bond, transport, food, and a few household items. This is a planning checklist — not personal financial advice.

# Why this matters
Running out of cash in week 2 makes every other task stressful. A simple list beats guessing.

# When to do it
Before you fly if you can, then again after you know your suburb and whether you share a house.

# Requirements
- Rough idea of your city (inner vs outer suburb)
- Whether you already have a room booked
- Your visa work rights (so you do not budget income you cannot legally earn yet)

# Step-by-step guide
1. List **must-pay in 14 days**: first night stay, SIM, transport card, groceries, any bond/holding deposit you have already agreed in writing.
2. Add **should-pay in 30 days**: extra kitchen items, a local GP visit if uninsured, licence checks if you will drive.
3. Keep a small cash buffer for card declines and laundry.
4. Use supermarket home brands and free council resources (libraries have Wi‑Fi).
5. Recheck the list after you know your commute — daily rideshare will blow the budget.

# Common mistakes
- Budgeting as if you will work 40 hours in week 1
- Buying a full furniture set before the lease is signed
- Ignoring transport: two long Uber rides can equal a weekly grocery shop

# FAQs
**Does AussieStart store your bank data?** No. This guide is a list, not a connected budget app.
**Where do I check typical rents?** Use major listing sites and treat ads as asking prices, not guarantees.

# Useful terms
- **Bond**: Refundable security, usually lodged with a state authority
- **Holding deposit**: Sometimes requested to pause a property — read the rules before you pay
